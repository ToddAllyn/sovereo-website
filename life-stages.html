Stage 1 · Usually about 50 to 64

Pre-retirement

You are earning more than ever and have savings to protect. Some of the biggest decisions of your life arrive in a few short years, and many are hard to undo.

The decisions

  • When to stop working, and whether to slow down first
  • When to claim your public pension
  • How to turn savings into a steady income
  • How to pay for healthcare until public cover starts
  • Where to live next, and what it will cost
  • Wills and the basics of passing on what you own

What can change the answer

  • Rising prices that shrink what your savings can buy
  • Interest rate changes that move savings, loans, and pensions
  • Changes to pension ages and pension rules
  • Stock and bond markets falling just before you retire
  • A weaker currency if you plan to spend in another one

Stage 2 · Usually about 65 and older

Retirement

You are living on what you have built. Health, housing, and what you pass on matter most, and there is less time to recover from a mistake.

The decisions

  • How much to spend each year so your money lasts
  • How to protect a fixed income from rising prices
  • How to get and pay for good healthcare
  • Whether to stay, downsize, or live somewhere cheaper
  • How to pass on money and property, including across borders

What can change the answer

  • Rising prices, especially for food, energy, and care
  • Currency swings if your pension is paid in one currency and spent in another
  • Changes to public health cover and pension payments
  • New tax or inheritance rules

Stage 3 · Usually about 30 to 49

Building

You are building a career, a home, and often a family. Decisions come often, and each one shapes the next twenty years.

The decisions

  • How secure your income is, and how to make it more secure
  • Whether to buy a home, and when
  • How and where to pay for your children’s education
  • How fast to pay down debt
  • How much to save for retirement now

What can change the answer

  • Interest rates that change mortgage and loan costs
  • New technology changing which jobs are safe
  • House prices and rents
  • Tax changes on income and savings

Stage 4 · Usually about 18 to 29

Starting out

You have time on your side and fewer assets to protect. The choices you make now about skills and income compound for decades.

The decisions

  • Whether a degree or course is worth its cost, at home or abroad
  • Which skills will still be paid well as technology changes
  • Your first job, and where the opportunities are growing
  • Starting to save, even in small amounts

What can change the answer

  • Automation and artificial intelligence changing entry-level work
  • The cost of study and student loans
  • Rent and living costs where the jobs are
  • Which countries are growing and hiring
