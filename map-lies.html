Why the map is wrong, in plain English

You do not need any background in geography. Here is the whole thing, simply.

What is the distortion?

Most world maps use the Mercator projection, drawn in 1569 for sea navigation. To keep compass directions straight it stretches everything as you move away from the equator. The further from the equator a country sits, the more the map inflates it, and near the poles the stretch becomes enormous.

The famous example

Greenland looks about the size of Africa on a standard map. In reality Africa is roughly fourteen times larger. Greenland sits far north, so the map balloons it; Africa straddles the equator, so it is drawn close to its true size. Drag any country north and it grows on screen; drag it to the equator and it shrinks to the truth.

What this tool does

When you move a country, the tool keeps its true size fixed and re-lays its real outline at the new location, then draws it on the same Mercator map. So the change you see on screen is the map's distortion, not the country changing. The area figure stays constant because the country never actually changes size.

Why an intelligence site cares

Because the same habit of mind that misreads a map misreads the world. A country that looks dominant may be smaller than you think, and a place you overlooked may be larger or more livable than the map suggests. Seeing clearly is the whole point of Sovereo.

How this works. The base map and every outline are drawn from the Natural Earth public dataset via the world-atlas project, with no map tiles and no API key. The map uses the Mercator projection, the same one that distorts size on standard world maps. When you move a country, each point of its outline is repositioned by the same true ground distance and bearing from the new center, which preserves real area while Mercator renders the apparent-size change. Area is computed from the outline's spherical geometry and shown in square kilometres and square miles; these are close approximations from simplified boundaries. For general education.
