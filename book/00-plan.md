## 1. The one sentence

You cannot control the ministry, the central bank, or the index. You can manufacture notice, and notice is the part of control that decides how your life goes.

## 2. The argument in five moves

1. Control over your own circumstances is not a preference. It is a measured input to how long and how well you live. Marmot found it in 17,530 civil servants who all had salaries, pensions and the same health service.
2. That finding is real, replicated, and smaller than its reputation. Stated honestly, it does not carry a book. What carries a book is the part of it that can be separated out and manufactured.
3. That part is predictability. de Berker and colleagues measured stress against uncertainty rather than against threat, and found stress peaked where the environment was least readable, not where the bad outcome was most likely. Uncertainty is expensive because it removes the ability to act in advance.
4. Notice, the interval between the date an instrument is published and the date it binds, is the measurable form of predictability. It is collapsing. Spain went from 182 days to one day on the same regulation, under the same ministry, in eighteen months.
5. Notice can be manufactured by a person, using methods that already exist and are not secret. The book teaches them, then holds itself to them.

## 3. Why this book and not the other one

Two books were available in the material.

The first is the executive Decision Integrity book outlined in January 2026, aimed at CEOs and boards, built around an audit standard and a simulation lab. It is a good B2B asset and it should stay alive as the practice-tier product that feeds the $1,450 Decision Audit.

The second is this one. It is the book the research actually supports, because the research has an original empirical finding in it. The notice ledger is not a restatement of anyone. It is a measurement nobody in this category has published. A trade book needs one thing that is true and new, and that is it.

This plan is for the second book. It absorbs the self-schema material from the first as its Part Three rather than discarding it.

## 4. Reader

Primary: the person who has started pricing exit. The four Sovereo reader tiers apply and are held constant across the book, as they are across every Sovereo product.

- **The Aspirant.** Deciding whether to go at all. Needs the stakes and the method.
- **The Retiree.** Fixed income, fixed address, single jurisdiction, no adjustment margin. Taleb's fragile system by construction. The most exposed reader in the book and the one the chapters must never mark "not relevant."
- **The Active Expat.** Already out, and therefore already inside the notice problem rather than reading about it.
- **The Legacy Builder.** Moving capital as well as a household, and thinking in generations.

Secondary: the practitioner who advises them. Cross-border planners, elder law attorneys, enrolled agents. This is the audience that buys the practice tier.

The book is not written for the executive suite. That reader gets the other book.

## 5. Hook and thesis, per the locked positioning

The house rule holds in the book exactly as it holds in a title or a bio. Lead narrow, pay off deep.

- **Hook.** Money and where to live. Chapter one opens on a household that lost 32 percent of its purchasing power in Mexico, and on the household in Ohio that lost 18.8 percent by deciding nothing at all.
- **Thesis.** Decision integrity across the determinants: income, education, health, belonging, legacy. Never lead with the framework. Always land on it.

## 6. Voice and constraints

- House style is locked. No em dashes. No AI linguistics. Neutral throughout.
- First person for orientation, third person for evidence. This mirrors the Brief, where the ORIENT paragraphs carry a stated read and the OBSERVE paragraphs carry the raw material.
- Every number traces to a primary source, in the notes, by name and date.
- Every claim that could flatter the author is stated with its limit attached. The honest deflation in chapter four is the credibility engine of the whole book.
- Politically neutral is not a posture, it is a structural requirement. Sen is the reason it survives contact with a mixed audience, and the reason is given in the text rather than asserted.

### PART ONE. THE STAKES
*Why where you live is a health variable, not a lifestyle variable.*

**Chapter 1. The Household That Decided Nothing**
Opens on the money door. Two households, one that moved to Mexico and lost 32 percent of purchasing power, one that stayed in Ohio and lost 18.8 percent. Establishes the rule that governs the whole book: never publish a cost without its alternative.
*Mentor: Thomas Sowell, the benchmark discipline and the trade-off.*
*Source: Sovereo purchasing power work, BIS real effective exchange rate series, June 2026.*

**Chapter 2. Seventeen Thousand Five Hundred and Thirty Civil Servants**
The Whitehall studies told straight. Everyone employed, salaried, pensioned, same health service. Lowest grade at 3.6 times the coronary mortality of the highest, and the gradient holding at every step including between the second rank and the first. Smoking, blood pressure, cholesterol, weight and sedentary lifestyle explaining no more than about a quarter of it. Whitehall II in The Lancet in 1997 naming low control at work as the largest contributor.
*Mentor: Michael Marmot, the empirical foundation.*

**Chapter 3. The Causes of the Causes**
From one cohort to a framework. The WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health, 2008. The six things that turn out to determine a life: income and opportunity, education, health and longevity, environment, lifestyle and belonging, legacy and sovereignty. Green space narrowing health inequalities. Social isolation carrying a mortality risk comparable to obesity. The chapter ends by naming the move the book is making: if these are determinants, and they vary by country, then choosing a country is choosing a set of determinants.
*Mentors: Marmot and the Commission; Mitchell and Popham; Holt-Lunstad.*

**Chapter 4. The Honest Deflation**
The chapter that buys the reader's trust by giving away the strongest version of the author's own case. The IPD-Work Consortium pooled 197,473 people and 2,358 incident coronary events, found a hazard ratio of 1.23 for job strain and a population attributable risk of 3.4 percent, and stated that addressing workplace stress would matter less than addressing smoking. MIDUS, 6,135 adults over 14 years, control beliefs at a hazard ratio of 0.87 per standard deviation, with the protective interaction appearing only among those low in education. Anyone selling control as the master variable of human health is overselling. This book does not.
*Mentors: Richards Heuer, the discipline of refutation. Walter Williams, the willingness to publish an inconvenient finding.*

**Chapter 5. What Can Actually Be Manufactured**
The pivot, and the intellectual centre of the book. Control and predictability separate. de Berker and colleagues, Nature Communications, 2016: subjective stress, pupil diameter, skin conductance and salivary cortisol all tracking irreducible uncertainty, peaking where the estimated probability of a shock was 50 percent, the point at which the environment is utterly unpredictable. Grupe and Nitschke on the functional account. Stress did not peak where the outcome was worst. It peaked where the environment was least readable. Nobody can hand you control of a foreign price level. Notice is the component of control that can be manufactured.
*Mentors: Amartya Sen, capability rather than resources, and the bridge that lets autonomy and health sit in one sentence. Epictetus and Seneca, the dichotomy of control and the praemeditatio malorum, which is the philosophical form of notice.*

### PART TWO. THE COLLAPSE
*The original finding. Notice is measurable, and it is shrinking.*

**Chapter 6. The Notice Ledger**
The book's proprietary exhibit. Nine instruments across seven jurisdictions, 2022 to 2026, each with its publication date, its date in force, its interval in days, and its mechanism. Spain 182 days. Colombia 90. Thailand about 107. The United States COLA about 38. Mexico's UMA 24. Colombia's wage decree 2. Portugal 3. Spain again, on the same regulation, 1. Italy, no published figure at all.

Four findings, none requiring a model:
1. Notice is uncorrelated with the size of the change.
2. Notice is collapsing within jurisdictions, not only across them. The Spain row is the persuasive one because it holds the legal system constant.
3. Indexation moves the surprise from the rule to the index. The rule gives the multiple. The gazette gives the multiplicand, in the last week of December.
4. Discretion produces the limiting case, where there is no interval to measure at all.

**Chapter 7. Risk You Can Price, Uncertainty You Cannot**
Knight's 1921 distinction, and the reason two countries with identical thresholds are not identical exposures. An indexed threshold published as a multiple of a gazetted wage is a risk with a knowable distribution. A threshold set by ministerial discretion with no published formula is uncertainty in the strict sense, and no analysis converts it into a risk. The commercial and personal consequence is the same: the first can be forecast, the second can only be monitored, and an honest product says which is which on its face.
*Mentor: Frank Knight. Supporting: Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony and Cass Sunstein on noise, because two consulates assessing identical files is not an analogy, it is the case.*

**Chapter 8. Seeing Like a State**
What a threshold is. States must render populations legible to govern them, and legibility requires simplification. A residency income floor converts a household with a history, a set of obligations and a tolerance for risk into one number a ministry can process. Then the number becomes the reality policy acts on. The chapter pairs Scott with Hayek deliberately and says so: two thinkers from opposite ends of the political spectrum, working different problems, arriving at the same finding. That convergence is the proof the method is not partisan.
*Mentors: James C. Scott and Friedrich Hayek. Supporting: Michael Polanyi, on the honest boundary of what any registry can hold, which is why consular variation is recorded rather than averaged away.*

**Chapter 9. Why States Do This**
The incentive underneath the announcement. Olson's stationary bandit setting a price high enough to screen and extract, low enough not to empty the country of the income it wants. Tilly on extraction applied to a mobile, wealthy, non-voting population that consumes services and has no political recourse, which explains both why it is priced and why nobody defends it. North on credible commitment, which is the rigorous reason a published indexation formula is worth more than a lower number set by decree. Machiavelli on reading the incentive rather than the announcement, which is why a finance ministry under pressure and an immigration ministry issuing reassurances are not in conflict.
*Mentors: Mancur Olson, Charles Tilly, Douglass North, Niccolò Machiavelli.*

**Chapter 10. The Macro Chain**
Why this is structural and not a run of bad years. The US aged dependency ratio moving from 0.322 in 2025 to 0.400 in 2050 on the Trustees' intermediate assumptions, and OASI depletion projected in the fourth quarter of 2032 with 78 percent of scheduled benefits payable after. The cleanest illustration in the book, which involves no rule change at all: the COLA formula is fully public, the announcement landed between 10 and 13 October for four consecutive years, and in 2025 the September CPI moved to 24 October. The formula did not change. The benefit did not change. Only the notice changed. Medicare not travelling, the Part B penalty at 10 percent per uncovered year for life, and the 2026 standard premium at $202.90 a month, which is roughly $2,435 a year for coverage that will not pay a claim abroad. And the currency refusing to obey the textbook: BIS real effective exchange rates running from Mexico at 140.34 to Japan at 65.30 against a 2020 base, with Murray and Papell showing the upper bounds of the 95 percent half-life confidence interval are infinite.

**Chapter 11. Exit, Voice, and the Price of Leaving**
Hirschman on the two responses to decline, and the interaction between them. Where exit is easy, voice atrophies. Where exit is blocked, voice intensifies. Loyalty delays exit long enough for voice to work. The reader of this book has largely stopped using voice and is pricing exit. Tiebout on jurisdictional sorting, with the retiree case stated precisely: a retiree consumes services without supplying labour, so receiving states face a mixed incentive, foreign pension income attractive and foreign health consumption not. That mixed incentive is why retiree visa policy oscillates rather than trends, and oscillation is what destroys predictability.
*Mentors: Albert Hirschman, Charles Tiebout.*

### PART THREE. THE SELF
*Why people do not act on notice they already have.*

**Chapter 12. The Schema That Decides Before You Do**
Self-schema as the organising idea. The stable set of beliefs a person holds about who they are, which determines which options are visible before any deliberation begins. The chapter is careful here: this is the section where books in this genre go soft, and it will not. The claim is structural rather than therapeutic. A self-schema restricts an option set, and a restricted option set is a decision exposure that exists before execution starts.
*Mentors: Herbert Simon, bounded rationality and satisficing, the accurate model of how the reader really decides. Supporting: schema work in the clinical literature, cited for the mechanism and not for the promise.*

**Chapter 13. The Four Exposures**
Drawn directly from the Decision Integrity Audit instrument, reduced from eight detected conditions to the four that matter to a household rather than a board.
1. **Restricted option sets.** The countries you never considered because of who you think you are.
2. **Timing distortions.** Reading a window as open because closing it would require a decision.
3. **Unverified assumptions.** The number you have never traced. Williams's habit, made personal.
4. **Identity pressure inside decisions.** The reason the strongest evidence loses to the weakest self-image.
Each carries a stop point: a short exercise the reader runs before continuing.

**Chapter 14. Why the Whole Category Lies to You, and Why It Cannot Stop**
Akerlof's lemons market, which is the economic theory of this entire industry and is fifty-six years old. A reader cannot evaluate the accuracy of relocation advice before acting on it, and in most cases cannot evaluate it for years after. Sellers know their own accuracy. Readers will not pay a premium for accuracy they cannot verify, so the sellers who remain are the ones with a second income stream from commissions. This is the predicted equilibrium of the market structure, not a moral failing of its participants. Akerlof also names the exit: lemons markets are rescued by institutions that make quality verifiable.
*Mentors: George Akerlof. Supporting: Ronald Coase, on why the reader does not simply assemble this themselves, and why the benefit only becomes obvious at the moment the option is gone.*

**Chapter 15. The Moral Permission, and Its Limit**
The chapter that answers the reader who has been told that leaving is unpatriotic or selfish. Rand supplies the moral case that a person's life is their own to dispose of and that the decision to leave is nobody's to veto. Then the limit is stated plainly in the same chapter, because the book states limits where it states claims: Rand's system assumes a rational actor with access to the relevant facts, and has almost nothing to say about what happens when the facts are unavailable, priced, or deliberately obscured, which is the condition the reader is actually in. Smith supplies what Rand does not, the impartial spectator, the imagined disinterested observer whose judgment a person consults to check their own. That figure is the whole of the independence claim.
*Mentors: Ayn Rand for the permission and not the method. Adam Smith for the division of labour that justifies outsourcing this research, and the impartial spectator that justifies doing it without commissions.*

### PART FOUR. THE METHOD
*How notice is manufactured. This is the part the reader paid for.*

**Chapter 16. Collect, Do Not Model**
The epistemology of everything that follows. Hayek in 1945: the knowledge required to coordinate an economy is not given to any single mind, it is dispersed, specific to particular circumstances of time and place, and much of it is never articulated. Applied here, the knowledge of what a Colombian residency threshold will be next January exists, in December, inside a Colombian ministry. It is specific to time and place. No model derives it. It can only be collected, from the source, on the day. This is why the method is a registry and not a model, and why the SITREP triangulates 105 feeds rather than reasoning from a worldview.
*Mentor: Friedrich Hayek. Supporting: Thomas Sowell, on which decision arrangements economise on knowledge and which squander it.*

**Chapter 17. Orientation Is the Whole Job**
Boyd, told correctly. The popular reading is that speed wins and the objective is to cycle faster than an opponent. Boyd's own emphasis was different, and he wrote it down in Organic Design for Command and Control: orientation, as the repository of genetic heritage, cultural tradition and previous experiences, is the most important part of the loop because it shapes the way we observe, decide and act. Orientation is the Schwerpunkt. The instruction to a reader is direct. Observation is a commodity, and the feeds deliver it free. Orientation is where a person earns their own money, because orientation is where accumulated position gets applied to what was observed. It is also the step a language model performs worst, because it requires a position built over time rather than a pattern retrieved on demand.
*Mentor: John Boyd.*

**Chapter 18. Say What You Mean by Probably**
Kent's essay, and the story that carries it. A 1951 national estimate used the phrase serious possibility about an invasion of Yugoslavia. Kent had meant roughly 65 to 35 in favour. His questioner had read it very considerably lower. When Kent polled his own drafters, the low man was thinking of about 20 to 80 and the high of 80 to 20. One phrase, in one estimate, read across a range from 20 percent to 80 percent by the people who wrote it. Kent's answer was a published table mapping words to odds, and every calibrated probability standard in use today descends from it. The chapter gives the reader the yardstick and requires them to use it on their own beliefs.
*Mentor: Sherman Kent.*

**Chapter 19. Argue Against Yourself First**
Heuer's inversion of the intuitive procedure. Focus on developing arguments against each hypothesis rather than trying to confirm them, because the most likely hypothesis is usually the one with the least evidence against it, not the one with the most evidence for it. The chapter teaches Analysis of Competing Hypotheses at household scale, and adds the two steps most people skip: report the relative likelihood of all hypotheses rather than only the most likely, and identify in advance the milestones that would indicate events taking a different course.
*Mentor: Richards Heuer.*

**Chapter 20. Write It Down and Let It Be Scored**
The close, and the standard the book submits to. Tetlock's twenty-year study, in which credentialed experts making political and economic forecasts performed barely better than chance, and the most confident and most famous performed worst. Accuracy improving markedly under four conditions: resolution criteria, probabilistic answers, scoring against outcomes, and public tracking over time. Brier's 1950 scoring rule and Murphy's 1973 partition into reliability, resolution and the uncertainty inherent in the events themselves, which is why a desk that says 50 percent about everything can be perfectly reliable and useless. Taleb's principle that a forecaster bearing no consequence for being wrong is not a forecaster.

Then the chapter turns the standard on the book's own category. The Sovereign Individual made a large, dated, falsifiable prediction, that the nation state would dissolve under pressure from digital technology and mobile capital. What happened instead is that states became substantially better at tracking, taxing and pricing mobile people. FATCA, the Common Reporting Standard, beneficial ownership registries and indexed residency thresholds are all instruments of a state apparatus that got stronger in exactly the period the book said it would dissolve. An author whose own founding category got a call that large backwards should be the first to say so, because that is the standard he intends to be held to.
*Mentors: Philip Tetlock, Glenn Brier and Allan Murphy, Nassim Nicholas Taleb, and the category's founders read as a cautionary case: Harry Schultz, W.G. Hill, Doug Casey, James Dale Davidson and William Rees-Mogg.*

## 8. Apparatus

The book ships as an installation rather than a read. Four devices, all drawn from working Sovereo practice.

1. **Stop points.** One short exercise at the end of each chapter, five minutes, run before continuing.
2. **The reader's own notice ledger.** Built across Part Two, one row per instrument that binds the reader's life, with publication date, date in force, interval and mechanism. This is the book's signature artifact.
3. **The probability yardstick.** Introduced in chapter 18 and used by the reader from that point to the end.
4. **The reader's forecast ledger.** Opened in chapter 20. Dated, scoreable, with the scoring dates written in before the reader knows the answers.

## 9. What the book must not do

- It must not claim control as the master variable of human health. Chapter 4 exists to prevent that.
- It must not sell a country. No compensation is accepted from any relocation, residency, immigration, banking or investment migration company, and the book says so on the copyright page. The reason is mechanical rather than moral: every one of those counterparties is paid when a household moves.
- It must not mark any chapter as not relevant to the Retiree. That failure is visible in the current daily product and must not survive into the book.
- It must not read as partisan. The Scott and Hayek convergence in chapter 8 and the Sen bridge in chapter 5 are the structural guarantees, and both are argued rather than asserted.
- It must not use an em dash.

## 10. Falsification

The book states in its own front matter what would show it to be wrong.

1. If notice intervals across the tracked jurisdictions widen rather than narrow over the next 36 months, the central empirical claim of Part Two fails.
2. If the reader's manufactured notice does not change any decision, the practical claim of Part Four fails, and the book has produced literature rather than intelligence.
3. If the forecast ledger opened in chapter 20 scores poorly in the high confidence bands, the method claim fails, and the next edition says so with the numbers.

1. Freeze this plan and the title.
