The Exit Path · Capital egress instrument

Whether the dollar comes back out

Every instrument in this category covers entry. Visa routes, purchase costs, yields, incorporation steps. Almost nobody covers exit, because exit is where the failures are, and failures do not sell relocation services. The Exit Path covers exit.

Specification version 1 Publication: living Companion to the Reference Basket
01 · What this measures

Egress failure is almost always caused at entry

The Reference Basket answers what a dollar buys in a country. The Exit Path answers whether the dollar comes back out.

The structural insight the instrument is built on is that the failure is rarely caused at the exit. It is caused at the entrance. A registration not filed. A structure chosen for convenience. An account opened at the wrong institution. Nothing appears to go wrong at the time, because nothing does go wrong at the time. The consequence surfaces five to ten years later, when the person tries to sell and leave, and by then it is irreversible.

The instrument's job is to surface those irreversible points before the money moves, not after.

That is the whole design. Everything below, the six gates, the status vocabulary, the source register, exists to find the point at which a right is quietly forfeited, and to name it while it can still be preserved.

02 · Structure

Six gates, in sequence

Each gate is answered per jurisdiction and per asset class. The gates are sequential, and the order matters more than any single rule: failing an earlier gate can block a later one even where the later rule is entirely permissive. A country with no exchange controls at all can still be a closed exit if the entry filing was never made.

1

Entry registration

Was the inbound capital required to be registered with a central bank, investment authority, or tax authority at the moment of entry, and what does that registration confer?

Fixed questions
  • Is registration of foreign investment mandatory, voluntary, or absent?
  • Which body registers it, and under what instrument?
  • What is the filing window, and is late filing curable or fatal?
  • Does registration confer repatriation rights, and are those rights lost without it?
  • Does the registration attach to the asset, the investor, or the transaction?
  • Are real estate, operating business equity, and portfolio holdings treated differently?

This is the single highest-value question in the instrument. In several roster jurisdictions the right to repatriate is conferred by an entry filing the buyer never hears about, because the person selling them the asset has no reason to mention it.

2

Holding period compliance

What must be filed, annually and continuously, to preserve the ability to exit?

Fixed questions
  • Local tax residency trigger, in days and in any secondary test.
  • Worldwide income reporting obligation once resident.
  • Worldwide asset or net worth reporting obligation once resident.
  • Annual foreign investment registration renewal or update requirement.
  • Beneficial ownership register filing obligation.
  • Consequence of a missed filing: penalty only, or loss of a right.
3

Conversion

Can local currency be converted to dollars, at what rate, and under what limits?

Fixed questions
  • Is there a single official rate, a multiple rate regime, or a parallel market?
  • Annual or per-transaction conversion limits, for individuals and for entities.
  • Documentation required to convert above threshold.
  • Is prior authorisation required, and from whom?
  • Practical time from request to settlement.
  • Does the country appear on any Financial Action Task Force list, and is correspondent banking affected?
4

Transfer

Can converted funds leave, and what is withheld on the way?

Fixed questions
  • Withholding rate on dividends, interest, royalties, and rental income paid to a non-resident.
  • Applicable United States treaty rate and the procedure to claim it, including whether a residency certificate is required in advance.
  • Is there a totalisation agreement covering social contributions? Where none exists, the double contribution exposure is stated explicitly.
  • Cross-border payment reporting thresholds.
  • Bank-level friction: documentation, wire limits, and whether the receiving United States institution will accept the corridor.
5

Disposal

What happens when the asset is sold?

Fixed questions
  • Capital gains treatment for a non-resident seller, rate and base.
  • Is gain computed on gross proceeds, or net of an indexed cost base?
  • Notary, registry, or buyer-side withholding at closing, and whether it is final or creditable.
  • Holding period affecting rate or exemption.
  • Foreign buyer or foreign seller surcharge.
  • Are sale proceeds subject to a separate repatriation authorisation, distinct from Gate 4?
  • Time from closing to funds available offshore.
6

Severance

What happens when the person stops being a tax resident, or dies holding the asset?

Fixed questions
  • Is there an exit tax on unrealized gains at cessation of residency?
  • Procedure and documentation to terminate tax residency, and whether a clearance certificate is required.
  • Continuing source-country obligations after departure.
  • Inheritance, estate, or succession tax on assets held by a non-resident.
  • Forced heirship rules affecting disposition by will.
  • Whether local law recognises a foreign trust, or the United States estate structure the person already has.
  • Bank account closure and final transfer friction.
03 · The headline output

Trap Points

Standing flag

The requirement you cannot go back and satisfy

A Trap Point is any requirement that must be satisfied at an earlier gate to preserve a right at a later gate, where late compliance is not curable.

Trap Points are carried separately from gate status because they behave differently. A restricted gate is a cost. A Trap Point is a door that has already closed behind the reader, usually without a notification, often years before the reader has any reason to look.

They publish as a standing list per jurisdiction, at the top of every country read, regardless of what else changed that week. Three to six per jurisdiction is the working expectation. Where a country read surfaces none, the finding is that the sourcing is incomplete, not that the jurisdiction is clean.

04 · Status marking

Five statuses, and nothing inferred

Each gate carries exactly one status, per jurisdiction and per asset class.

Open

No restriction beyond ordinary documentation.

Conditional

Permitted subject to a filing, authorisation, or threshold that is obtainable.

Restricted

Permitted, but with a material cost, delay, or quantitative limit. The cost is stated.

Blocked

Not permitted, or permitted only in circumstances the reader will not meet.

Unverified

Could not be sourced to standard. Published as unverified, never inferred.

The fifth status is the one that keeps the other four honest. Where a rule cannot be traced to a primary instrument, the cell publishes as Unverified rather than being filled from a plausible secondary summary. An instrument that never admits a gap is an instrument that is guessing somewhere.

05 · The American side

The United States layer

Half the egress problem sits on the American side, and the category almost never covers it, because it is unglamorous and it is the seller's least favourite subject. This layer is reported per structure, not per country.

Design rule. Every Gate 1 entry structure states its United States reporting consequence in the same table row. Separating the two is what causes the failure in the first place, and an instrument that reports them in different chapters reproduces the error it exists to prevent.

06 · Source register

The spine, and why one document does most of the work

Every endpoint is verified by hand. Foreign exchange regimes change by circular rather than by statute, and circulars do not announce themselves.

Gates 3 and 4

IMF Annual Report on Exchange Arrangements and Exchange Restrictions

The authoritative record of exchange and capital controls by country. Annual, narrative, written for central bankers. The single most underused document in the category, and it answers the conversion and transfer gates for every country on the roster.

Gate 1, partial

OECD FDI Regulatory Restrictiveness Index

Sectoral foreign ownership limits, read as a map of where a restriction is likely to sit, never as the authority for the rule itself.

Cross-check only

World Bank Business Ready

Firm lifecycle topics including business entry, taxation, dispute resolution, and insolvency. National aggregate. Used as a cross-check, never as sole authority for a specific rule.

Gate 3 friction

Financial Action Task Force

Public statements and the increased-monitoring list, which drive correspondent banking friction whatever the domestic rule permits.

Gate 4 rates

Bilateral treaty texts and protocols

Taken from both the source country tax authority and the United States Treasury. A treaty rate claim cites the article, not a summary table.

United States layer

Internal Revenue Service guidance

Statute and official guidance only.

Per country, the register runs to the primary body for each gate: the central bank foreign exchange regulation, the investment registration authority, the tax authority, the land or commercial registry, the notary association, the immigration authority, the national gazette, and the treaty text.

07 · Update triggers

Trigger driven, not calendar driven

The Exit Path does not run on a publication calendar. It runs on events, monitored weekly in the Class A slot alongside currency. Any one of these opens a re-read of the affected gate.

  • Central bank foreign exchange circular or resolution.
  • Capital control decree or emergency measure.
  • Tax reform bill filed, passed, or struck down.
  • Constitutional or supreme court ruling on a tax or exchange measure.
  • Treaty protocol signed or ratified.
  • Financial Action Task Force listing change.
  • Investment registration rule change.
  • Residency programme opened, amended, or closed.
  • Annual release of the IMF exchange arrangements report.

Status discipline, carried from the Reference Basket. A measure moves through five states, and only one of them enters the instrument.

Announced→Filed→Passed→In force→Struck down

A rule enters the Exit Path only when in force. Announcements are reported and flagged, never scored. This is the same discipline that governs the forecast ledger, and it is the reason the instrument moves later than the headlines and is right more often than they are.

09 · The position

It states rules and sources them. It does not advise.

No structure recommendations. No entity selection guidance. No language telling a reader where to register or what to hold it through. The output is what the rule is, where it is written, when it changed, and what it blocks.

That line is not caution. It is the position. Sovereo does not source deals, does not take fees on transactions, and does not advise on structures, which is exactly why its account of the rules can be trusted. An operator who sells offshore structures cannot publish a neutral account of when those structures backfire, because the same page would have to say that what they sold you was the Trap Point.

Standing line, carried on every issue. This is a published account of rules in force. It is not legal or tax advice, and no reader should act on it without qualified counsel in both jurisdictions.
