Scientific Basis & Validation

A tool built on tested science, not opinion.

Sovereo Best-Fit Countries is our short quiz that matches countries to your priorities. It works the way experts handle big, complex choices. It measures conditions that research links to wellbeing. It asks what truly matters to you. Then it combines the two with an open method built on proven rules. This page explains what it measures, how it decides, what is proven, and what still needs testing.

01 · What it measures

What it measures comes from established health science

Best-Fit Countries does not rank "nice places." It scores the social determinants of wealth and health (SDOH for short): the conditions around you that decades of health research link to how long and how well you live. The Whitehall studies of British government workers found that the risk of dying rises step by step as rank at work falls. The cause was not only income, but how much control people had over their lives. In 2008 the World Health Organization's Commission on Social Determinants of Health, led by Sir Michael Marmot, named these conditions the "causes of the causes" of health. The six areas the tool uses match this research. They also match the OECD Better Life Index, the main wellbeing measure that governments use to compare countries.

02 · How it decides

Scoring on several factors, using proven rules

Comparing countries means weighing many goals that pull against each other. Decision science has a standard method for this: Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA), which scores each option on several factors and combines them. Its core theory is Multi-Attribute Utility Theory (MAUT). Keeney and Raiffa's 1976 book Decisions with Multiple Objectives set out the basic rules for adding up scores on each factor, using the weights the person chooses. MCDA is not a marketing trick. It is the standard method for judging new medical treatments, set out in guidance from the ISPOR MCDA Emerging Good Practices Task Force. It is also used in medicine, public policy and business planning.

Best-Fit Countries follows the method's required steps. First, pick factors that do not overlap. Second, score each country on each factor from hard data. Third, ask the person how much each factor matters. Fourth, combine these into an open fit score. Hard limits (budget, visa route, language) work as filters, because a high score elsewhere cannot make up for them. Most important, the result depends on how well a country fits your stated priorities and limits, not on an average ranking. So a remote worker who wants low tax and a family who puts safety first get very different countries.

03 · How it reads your priorities

Asking what matters to you, using tested formats

How much each factor counts comes from you. We ask using stated-preference methods (ways of asking people what they prefer): discrete-choice experiments, conjoint analysis and best-worst scaling. These are the standard tools for measuring what people want in health economics and market research. Large reviews of these methods find that they measure priorities well. Best-Fit Countries makes you choose between options instead of rating everything as important. Tradeoffs show what you will really give up. That makes the result honest and useful rather than flattering.

04 · Where the data comes from

Every factor comes from a trusted data source

Country scores are not our guesses. Each factor is calculated from an established dataset, checked by other experts or published by a major institution. We then put every measure on the same 0 to 100 scale so they can be compared:

  • UNDP Human Development Report 2025, income, education and life-expectancy indices
  • World Bank, governance indicators (political stability), homicide rates, PPP price levels
  • Yale Environmental Performance Index 2024, 58 weighted environmental indicators
  • WHO, healthcare coverage and outcomes
  • Heritage Index of Economic Freedom 2026, tax burden, property rights, financial, business and investment freedom
  • Chinn-Ito Index (KAOPEN) 2023 update, how freely the law lets money move in and out of a country, the basis of the Capital Repatriation exit test
  • EF English Proficiency Index 2025; UCLA Williams Institute Global Acceptance Index

Alongside the six pillars, the index has a Capital and Enterprise lens: a separate score for readers whose money crosses borders. It scores three parts. Openness to money from abroad and ease of doing business come from Heritage. Capital repatriation (taking your money back out of a country) comes from the Chinn-Ito index. Repatriation counts the most, because the ability to take your money out is the first thing to fail in a crisis. We report this lens apart from the six-pillar overall score. A good place to live and a good place for your money are different questions.

05 · How it is validated

Checked by review, not by collecting your data

We check the tool with expert review and clear reasoning, not with a large trial on real users. This is on purpose. A trial would mean collecting sensitive personal data, with the legal and ethical duties that brings under privacy laws such as GDPR. Instead, we earn trust with evidence any reviewer can check directly. And the tool is built so that it collects no personal data at all.

Established. The tool is built from tested parts: a list of factors taken from health research on social conditions, a scoring method (MCDA/MAUT) based on proven rules and written good practice, question formats shown to measure priorities well, and data from recognised indices. Its logic is open, and anyone can repeat it and get the same result. We have also tested it with sample profiles: very different people get the different results you would expect.

Privacy by design. The tool never asks for your net worth. It estimates your financial position indirectly. It runs on your own device and does not keep data that identifies you. Because it does not collect or store sensitive personal data, it falls outside the heaviest duties of privacy laws such as GDPR and CCPA. It is an educational tool to help you decide: structured guidance, not a guarantee. We say this plainly because trust is what we offer.

06 · The validation framework

Reviewed, logical, open to checking

  • Expert content review: a panel of experts (using a structured method called modified Delphi) checks the factors, weights and country scores against published research.
  • Link to evidence: an up-to-date map linking every question and factor to the research or tested tool it comes from.
  • Sample profiles: a written set of typical made-up people confirms that different people get the different results you would expect. Anyone can repeat it, and it uses no personal data.
  • Advisor check: experienced advisors who work across borders judge whether each sample shortlist makes sense for the profile. This check collects no user data.
  • Openness and repeatability: the same answers always give the same result, every change is recorded, and each result can be traced back to its inputs.
  • Yearly update: we re-score countries as the public data updates, and record every change in a log.
