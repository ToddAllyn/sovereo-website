How to read this, in plain English

You do not need any financial knowledge to use this. Here is what it means.

"The richest X% in the world"

Imagine everyone on Earth lined up from the lowest earner to the highest. This tells you where you stand in that line. Your place in the line is called a percentile. "Top 3%" means only 3 out of every 100 people worldwide earn more than you. Most people are surprised how high they rank, because incomes in much of the world are very low.

Why we ask your household and family size

A given income supports one person far more comfortably than a family of five. So we take your whole household's income and adjust it for how many people it has to cover, which gives a fairer picture of each person's standard of living.

Why it is not just about the exchange rate

Money buys different amounts in different places. A modest income can pay for a comfortable life where prices are low. We use World Bank "purchasing power" data, which measures what money actually buys in each country. So your rank shows real living standards, not just a currency conversion. The "in US dollars" line is a simple conversion shown for reference, and we tell you the exact rate used.

Treat it as a guide

These are careful estimates built from global data, meant to give you perspective, not an exact accounting of your finances.

How this is calculated. First we adjust your household income for household size. We use the OECD square-root scale: income divided by the square root of the number of people. Then we convert it into 2021 "international dollars" using World Bank purchasing power parity (PPP) factors. PPP is a way of comparing money across countries by what it buys locally, not by the exchange rate. We then place that figure on a ranking of all incomes worldwide, from the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform (2024, via Our World in Data). It covers about 97 percent of the world's population. The "at today's exchange rate" line uses live market rates and is shown for reference only; the ranking itself uses purchasing power. All figures are best estimates, not exact facts. World population taken as 8.2 billion.
