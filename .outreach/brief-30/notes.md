# The connection request note

## The note

> [First], [specific thing]. I made that move with my family, and now publish a
> free daily on the money and border side of it, sourced and dated. Every
> morning, no cost, no pitch.

177 characters empty. Worst realistic fill lands at 236, so every version of it
clears the 300 cap with room left.

Three things are doing the work:

- **[specific thing]** is the whole note. It is the only proof you read their
  profile, and it is what gets the accept. Never send this without it.
- **"I made that move with my family"** is the line nobody else in this space
  can write. Brokers and listicle publishers sell a move they never made. Keep
  it, even when you are cutting for length.
- **"no cost, no pitch"** removes the only reason to decline. An invitation that
  smells like a sales sequence gets declined by exactly the senior people you
  want. Saying the quiet part kills the objection before it forms.

### The five swap lines for [specific thing]

Never send the same fill twice. Match it to what the profile actually shows.

| Recipient | Fill |
|---|---|
| Posted about leaving or moving | `saw your post on [their exact situation]` |
| Already living abroad | `you have been in [city] since [year]` |
| Pre-retiree with assets | `[number] years at [company] puts retirement in calculation range` |
| Cross-border role or investor | `you run [region] for [company], so you see this earlier than most` |
| Engaged one of your posts | `your note on the [topic] post was a sharper read than most` |

### Filled, so the shape is clear

> Maria, saw your post on selling the house in Austin before the visa is
> approved. I made that move with my family, and now publish a free daily on the
> money and border side of it, sourced and dated. Every morning, no cost, no
> pitch.

> David, 22 years at Raytheon puts retirement in calculation range. I made that
> move with my family, and now publish a free daily on the money and border side
> of it, sourced and dated. Every morning, no cost, no pitch.

### When they accept

The note does not include a link, on purpose. Links in invitations suppress
acceptance. Send this the same day they accept, and not before:

> Thanks [First]. Here it is: mail.sovereo.com
>
> What are you closest to deciding right now, the country, the money, or the
> timing?

That question is the handoff into the Track A opener in `messages.md`. The note
gets the connect. This gets the conversation. The conversation is what converts
to the Brief.

## Variants, if you want to rotate wider than the five fills


LinkedIn caps the invitation note at 300 characters. That cap is the whole design
problem. You get one specific line about them, one line that earns the read, and
one door. Nothing else fits, and nothing else is needed.

Two operational notes before the copy:

- If you are on a free LinkedIn account, personalized invites are now rationed
  per month. Check your remaining count before you plan a week around them. A
  request with no note still performs well, so spend the notes on the highest
  scoring names and send the rest bare.
- Do not put a link in the note. Links in invitations suppress acceptance and
  read as a pitch. Name the SITREP, let them find it, or send it after they
  accept. The one exception is the warm variant below where they already know it.

---

## Rule: the note sells the SITREP, not the Brief

A connection request carrying a $47 a month ask converts near zero and gets
reported. The free daily is the door. Sequence is: note earns the connect, the
opener earns the second reply, the sample earns the offer.

Only send a Brief-direct note to someone who already reads the SITREP or has
engaged your posts twice. Those are at the bottom.

Character counts, brackets unfilled: N1 223, N2 226, N3 234, N4 210, N5 184,
N6 234, N7 199. That leaves 66 to 116 characters of headroom for the brackets to
expand. A long situation detail can add 30 or 40, so recount after filling rather
than trusting the template count. LinkedIn truncates silently, it does not warn.

## SITREP door, longer variants

Rotate them. Fill every bracket with something real or do not send.

### N1, they posted about leaving or moving
> [First], saw your post on [their exact situation]. Most of what gets written
> about that move is written by people who never made it. I did, with my family,
> and I publish a free daily read on the money and border side of it.

### N2, they already live abroad
> [First], you have been in [city] since [year], so you already know the research
> does not match the ground. I run a free daily on cross-border money and
> migration, sourced and dated. Would like to follow your read on [country].

### N3, pre-retiree with assets
> [First], [number] years at [company] means retirement is a calculation rather
> than a someday. The number almost nobody runs is what that income is worth in
> the currency they plan to spend it in. I publish a free daily on exactly that.

### N4, cross-border role or investor
> [First], you watch [region] for [company], so you see this earlier than most. I
> publish a free daily on capital, border and migration events, sourced and
> dated. Connecting because I would value your read on it.

### N5, they engaged a post already
> [First], your note on the [topic] post was a sharper read than most. The daily
> version of that is free and goes out every morning. Connecting so I can see
> what you are working through.

## Brief direct, two variants. Warm only.

Send these only to someone already on the SITREP list or who has engaged twice.

### N6, existing SITREP reader
> [First], you have been reading the SITREP a while. The weekly Brief is the next
> rung, one dated call a week, scored in public so the record is auditable. I
> would rather show you an issue than describe it. Connecting so I can send one.

### N7, they asked a question you answered
> [First], following up on [their question]. The full trace on that ran in this
> week's Brief, exhibits and the dated trigger included. Connecting so I can send
> you the issue rather than retype it here.

## The DM version, for people already connected

Same job, no character cap, still short. This replaces the note when the
connection already exists.

### D-N1
> [First], [one specific real thing from their profile or post].
>
> The part that catches most people at that stage is [one insight from the bank
> in messages.md].
>
> I put that kind of thing out every morning, free, at mail.sovereo.com. Worth a
> look if you are working through [their decision].

### D-N2, shorter and better for a busy senior name
> [First], saw [specific thing]. I write a free daily on cross-border money and
> migration for people making exactly that call. mail.sovereo.com if useful.
>
> What are you closest to deciding?

The question at the end is the only reason D-N2 outperforms D-N1. A note that
ends in a door gets a signup. A note that ends in a question gets a conversation,
and a conversation is what converts to the Brief.

---

## Check before you send

- Under 300 characters, counted, not estimated.
- One specific detail that could only be about this person.
- No link in an invitation note.
- Names the free daily, not the price.
- No em dash, no AI tells, neutral tone.
- Ends in a door or a question, never in "I would love to connect and add value".
