# The eight offer posts

Two a week for four weeks. These are the posts that point at the Brief rather
than the free SITREP. The other five posts each week stay on pure value and the
SITREP, because a feed that only sells stops travelling.

Cadence rule: one post a day, never two. Two posts in a day split the reach of
both. Comment for ten minutes after posting, that is where the lift comes from.

The first two lines are everything. They are what shows before "see more".
Lead with a number, a claim, or a tension. Never with a category label.

---

## Week 1

### Post 1, the mechanism
Hook:
> Brent fell 40 percent off its wartime high. Gold broke under $4,000. Yuan
> settlement slid on the CIPS rail. Three different markets, three different
> stories in the press.
>
> It was one event.

Body: trace the single catalyst through all three prices. Name the date. Show
that the correlation is the signal and the individual price is noise. End on the
dated trigger that decides whether the relief holds.

Close: name the Brief as where the full trace and the scored call live. Link in
the first comment, not in the post.

### Post 2, the ledger
Hook:
> I publish every forecast I make with a probability and a date, and I mark it
> correct or wrong in public on that date.
>
> The misses stay up. That is the uncomfortable part, and it is the whole point.

Body: most people in this space never get scored, so nothing they said last year
can be checked. Explain what the Reckoning is. Point at a call that went wrong
and what it taught.

Close: the Brief is where the calls are made before they are scored. Annual is
$470, two months free.

## Week 2

### Post 3, tactical value with an offer tail
Hook:
> The residency visa income threshold you researched last year is not the number
> you will apply against.
>
> It is indexed. It resets. People qualify in their spreadsheet and fail at the
> consulate.

Body: the mechanism, the reset timing, two named countries with the current
figures and the source. Practical and checkable.

Close: this is the kind of thing the Brief catches on the week it moves, not the
year after.

### Post 4, where the free line ends
Hook:
> The free daily tells you what moved.
>
> It will not tell you what to do about it, and I think it is worth being honest
> about why.

Body: awareness against decision. The SITREP is built for the first, the Brief
for the second. Describe the six part structure of an issue in plain terms. No
pressure, just the line drawn clearly.

Close: both links. Free first, paid second. Let people self-select.

## Week 3

### Post 5, the life, with photos
Hook:
> Four years ago I was running numbers for an institution. Now I run them for my
> own family, from [city], in a currency I do not get paid in.
>
> That last part is the one nobody warns you about.

Body: the real moment. What the move actually cost and what it returned. Photos
carry this post. Keep the text under it short.

Close: soft. The SITREP link. No hard offer on this one.

### Post 6, the track record
Hook:
> [Number] forecasts made. [Number] scored. [Number] correct.
>
> Here is the one I got most wrong, and what it cost anyone who followed it.

Body: pull the real numbers from the Reckoning ledger. Do not round them up. Walk
through the miss honestly and what changed in the method because of it.

Close: the strongest offer post of the four weeks. A record you can audit is the
only reason to pay for a forecast. $470 annual.

## Week 4

### Post 7, contrarian
Hook:
> Every "best countries to retire abroad" list ranks the same nine places.
>
> Not one of them tells you that the prescription you take every day is not
> available in six of them.

Body: what the lists leave out because they are written by people selling
transactions to millionaires. Cost is the easy factor. Health access, legal
standing, currency exposure, and exit cost are the ones that decide whether the
move holds.

Close: the Brief covers the audience the incumbents skip.

### Post 8, the direct close
Hook:
> I am going to do something I normally do not, and just ask.

Body: short. Who the Brief is for, in three specific lines. Who it is not for, in
two. The price, both options, stated plainly. The cancel-in-two-clicks line.

Close: the link, in the post this time, not the comment. This is the one post in
four weeks where that is correct.

## The rule that makes all eight work

Ten minutes of substantive comments on ideal-client posts, every day, after
posting. Not "great post". A real addition that another reader stops on. That is
what lifts reach, and it surfaces the warm names that go into tomorrow's tracker.
