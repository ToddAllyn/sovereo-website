The 15 highest-scoring countries of 2026

The Sovereo Index is our yearly score for every country. It rates all 195 on six things that shape a long, healthy, well-off life. Here are the fifteen that score highest, what they share, and why the best country for you may not be among them.

Updated 2 July 2026 · Built from the 2026 Sovereo Index

Most "best countries" lists rank the richest or the cheapest. The Sovereo Index measures something harder and more useful: how well a place gives you what decides how long, how healthy, and how well-off your life will be. Every country gets a score from 0 to 100 on six areas, and the Index combines them. The result is not a list of the richest economies. It lists the places that best combine income, health, environment, belonging, and the rule of law (laws that apply fairly to everyone, including the government).

What the leaders share

The pattern is not wealth alone. It is balance. The countries at the top rarely lead any single area. They simply have no weak one. They pair long, healthy lives with courts and governments that work, clean air, and societies that let a newcomer belong. An oil-rich state can pay high incomes and still score poorly here. Money does not make up for a harsh climate, a closed culture, or weak rule of law. The Index rewards the places that get all six areas roughly right at once.

The six areas

Each country is scored on Income (whether what you earn keeps its value), Education, Health and Longevity (how long and how healthy people live), Environment, Belonging (how a society treats people who arrive), and Legacy and Sovereignty (the rule of law and how lasting your rights are). A separate Capital and Enterprise lens scores each country on whether your money can get in, earn, and come back out, the part most country guides skip. The method rests on well-known research into how daily conditions shape health, from the Whitehall studies of British civil servants to the World Health Organization’s commission on the subject. You can read the full method on the Science page.

A country can score high as a place to live and poorly as a place to keep money, or the reverse. Argentina rates well for lifestyle, yet near the bottom on getting money back out. Singapore scores at the top on both. A single "best countries" number hides that difference. The Index is built to show it.

Why the best country for you may not be on this list

This is the honest part. The table above is a neutral ranking, built for no one in particular. It is not a recommendation. The right country is never the top of a world list. It is the top of yours. A retiree living on a fixed public pension (such as US Social Security), a remote worker trying to pay less tax, and a family focused on safety and schools have three different problems. They point to three different places. For a remote worker paid in US dollars who wants lower tax and an open culture, the answer is often Portugal or Uruguay, not Liechtenstein. For someone who wants to earn in a strong currency, spend in a cheaper one, and raise a family, it can be a country in the middle of this list.
