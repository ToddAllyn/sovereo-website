Research standard

The rules for what Sovereo publishes, how we decide how sure we are, and how we fix mistakes in public.

Research is only useful when you can tell apart checked facts, our judgment, what we do not know, and what we sell.

Checking facts

The more a claim matters, the more sources must confirm it. If only one source says something, we name that source and mark it unconfirmed, or we leave it out. Official data shows what a government or agency measured or did. It does not prove every reading of those numbers.

Truly separate sources

Ten reports that repeat the same original claim count as one source, not ten. We compare government, business, official, expert, regional, and independent sources against each other. No type of source is trusted automatically.

Impact before newness

A new announcement is not more important just because it is new. An older story stays important while its effects on people, institutions, money, or business keep growing, build on each other, or near a point where you must decide.

Evidence against us

We test every main judgment against the strongest case that it is wrong. Sources that disagree stay visible. We do not ignore a factual report just because it does not fit our view.

Neutral and even-handed

We hold governments, companies, campaign groups, and individuals to the same standard of proof. Sovereo describes what people do, what drives them, who is affected, and what it costs. We do not use any political side's moral language.

Research first, sales after

What we sell never shapes what we report, how sure we say we are, or what we conclude. Sovereo does not publish analysis about any deal or money flow that a related company is involved in. Any offer to subscribe or buy comes after the research.

How sure we are, and how much it matters

HighReal effects are already happening, the evidence is strong, and the impact on you is hard or costly to undo.
MediumThere is a believable path from the event to you, but how big, how soon, or how it plays out is still partly unclear.
LowIt is worth watching, but the evidence so far does not show wide or immediate effects.

Public record

Corrections

When a published fact is wrong in a way that matters, we fix that issue and note the date and what changed. We never quietly erase what we said before. Every forecast (a dated prediction) stays in our public ledger, our open record of forecasts, whether it turned out right or wrong.

You can send us a correction. Include the claim, the date of the issue, and a source that supports you. We will examine it as evidence, but we cannot promise our judgment will change.
