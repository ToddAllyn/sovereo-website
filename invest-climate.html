Where is your money actually welcome?

Some countries make it easy to invest, keep, and move money. They have open markets, fair rules, and stable governments. Others make it hard. Compare how welcoming each country is to investors and businesses, scored from named public data.

How to read this, in plain English

You do not need any finance background. Here is the whole thing, simply.

What does the score measure?

How friendly a country is to investors and business. Can foreigners own things and move money freely? Do the courts protect what you own? How heavy are the rules and taxes? How stable is the government? A high score means your money is welcome and protected. A low score means delays, limits, or risk.

What "capital openness" and "stability" add

When you pick a country, we also show its capital openness (how freely money can cross its border in and out) and its political stability. A country can look attractive but trap your money when you try to take it out. Or it can be open but unstable. Both matter to anyone with money there.

Why it matters

Where you put your money decides how safely it grows and whether you can get it back. Open, well-run markets protect it and let it grow. Closed or unstable ones can freeze it, tax it, or shrink its value. This is one of the first questions to ask whenever your money crosses a border.

Where this comes from. The score is the Capital & Enterprise part of the Sovereo Index, worked out from named public data, including the Heritage Foundation Index of Economic Freedom, the Chinn-Ito capital-account openness index, and World Bank data on government quality and prices, using a fixed, published method. Capital openness comes from the Chinn-Ito measure of how freely money moves across borders. Political stability comes from the Index’s measures of government quality. Scores run from 0 to 100 and cover 180 countries. This is an educational research tool, not investment advice. Check the details with a licensed professional before you put money in.
