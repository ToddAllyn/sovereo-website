# Sovereo Positioning

The source of truth for what Sovereo is, who it serves first, and how every piece of work is built. When site copy, a product, or a post conflicts with this page, this page wins.

## What Sovereo is

Sovereo is a global intelligence research company. It gives people quality information so they can make quality decisions about their lives, wherever they live and whatever country their life touches.

We track macro events (geopolitics, policy, interest rates, inflation, currencies, technology, demographics, institutions) and trace each one down to the micro choices a person actually faces in five domains:

1. **Income**: earning, saving, investing, protecting purchasing power.
2. **Education**: what to learn, what it costs, and what it returns.
3. **Health**: access, cost, and the choices that change outcomes.
4. **Lifestyle**: where and how to live, and what that costs.
5. **Legacy**: what is kept, passed on, and protected.

This applies at every life stage and in every country.

## What Sovereo is not

Sovereo is not a relocation company. Moving is one possible answer to some decisions, and our country data and tools remain useful for that answer. It is never the premise. A piece that starts with "where should you move" is framed wrong. It should start with the event and the decision it creates.

Sovereo is also not a US publication with a world section. It is global by design, and no single country is the default reader.

## The method: economics as the benchmark

Economics is the study of choices under scarcity. Every Sovereo analysis is judged against it. The standard chain:

**Macro event → mechanism → country exposure → life stage exposure → the choice → the tradeoff → the signal to watch**

Each piece names:

- **The event**: what moved, sourced and dated.
- **The mechanism**: how it transmits (prices, rates, rules, incentives, supply, currency).
- **Where it lands**: which countries and regions feel it, and how differently.
- **Who it touches**: which life stages and which of the five domains.
- **The choice**: the specific decision a reader faces, stated plainly.
- **The tradeoff**: the opportunity cost of each option, including doing nothing.
- **Reversibility**: whether the choice can be undone, and what it costs to wait.
- **The signal**: the next observable event that would change the judgment.

Core tools of the lens: opportunity cost, incentives, marginal thinking, time horizon and discounting, risk and irreversibility, and information gaps.

## The country layer: where macro becomes micro

The same event lands differently depending on where a person lives, earns, saves, and holds citizenship. A rate rise in Washington means a mortgage payment in Toronto, an import bill in Lagos, and a currency loss for a retiree in Lisbon paid in dollars. The country is the lens that turns a global event into a personal consequence.

That is why the country products sit at the center of Sovereo, not at the edge:

- **The Sovereo Index** scores 195 nations on six pillars that map directly onto the five domains:

  | Index pillar | Domain |
  |---|---|
  | Income and Opportunity | Income |
  | Education | Education |
  | Health and Longevity | Health |
  | Environment | Health, Lifestyle |
  | Lifestyle and Belonging | Lifestyle |
  | Legacy and Sovereignty | Legacy |

  The **Capital and Enterprise** lens stays separate, for readers whose money crosses borders.
- **Country dossiers** are the full read on one jurisdiction: its current conditions, its exposure to live macro events, and what that means for each domain and life stage.
- **The Atlas and map tools** show exposure across countries at a glance.
- **Best-Fit Countries** weights the pillars to one person's situation.

Every country product answers the same question: given what is moving in the world, what does living, earning, or holding assets here mean for your decisions?

## Who we serve

### The reader

Anyone making a consequential life decision that global events can change. In practice the core reader has one or more of these:

- **Income or savings exposed to more than one currency** (remote work, foreign pensions, overseas investments, remittances).
- **Family or assets across borders** (children studying abroad, parents in another country, property abroad, dual citizenship).
- **A decision where the country is a variable** (where to retire, where to educate children, where to base a business, where to hold assets).
- **A home country whose conditions are shifting** (inflation, currency weakness, policy change, instability) and a need to know what that means for them.

People who never cross a border still belong here: their prices, rates, jobs, and pensions are shaped by events far away.

### Ranked by decision value

Coverage is ranked by **decision value**, not headcount. Decision value asks how much is at stake when people in a group decide, weighted by:

- **Size**: how many people are in the group.
- **Stakes**: income and assets exposed to the decision.
- **Irreversibility**: how hard the decision is to undo.
- **Frequency**: how often consequential decisions arrive.
- **Willingness to pay**: how much the group already spends on advice and information.

### Life stages

Stages are defined by the decisions people face, not a fixed age, because retirement ages, pension systems, and education paths differ by country. Ages are typical ranges.

| Rank | Life stage | Why it ranks here | Decisions we lead with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-retirement (about 50 to 64)** | Peak income, large and growing assets, and the most irreversible decisions of a lifetime arrive in a short window. High spend on advice. | When to stop working, when to claim a public pension, how to move savings into income, how to cover healthcare until public coverage starts, where to live next, estate basics. |
| 2 | **Retirement (about 65 and older)** | Holds the largest share of household wealth in most developed economies. Decisions on health, housing, and legacy carry high stakes and little room to recover. | Drawdown and inflation protection, currency risk on a fixed income, healthcare cost and access, housing and location, estate and cross-border transfer. |
| 3 | **Building (about 30 to 49)** | Large population and heavy decision load (family, housing, career), but less wealth at stake and lower spend on research. | Career and income durability, housing, children's education and where to get it, debt, early retirement saving. |
| 4 | **Launch (about 18 to 29)** | Large group with long time horizons, but low assets and low willingness to pay. Serve with free tools and the SITREP; build the relationship early. | Education return on investment at home or abroad, first income, skills versus automation, where opportunity is growing. |

Headcount matters, but a smaller stage with more at stake ranks above a larger stage with less.

### Markets

Decision value also applies across geography. Content is written for a global reader, but emphasis follows where decision value is concentrated:

1. **English-reading, high-wealth markets**: United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland. Highest willingness to pay and the largest pools of retirement assets.
2. **Internationally mobile readers everywhere**: expatriates, retirees abroad, globally employed professionals, and diaspora with ties to two countries.
3. **High-wealth and fast-growing markets where English is a working language**: Gulf states, Singapore, India, South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Philippines, and others.
4. **Readers in countries under macro stress**: where inflation, currency, or policy risk makes good information most valuable per decision.

When an example is needed, rotate countries and name several. Do not default to one country's institutions. Say "public pension" before naming Social Security, CPP, or the State Pension; say "public health coverage" before naming Medicare or the NHS.

Revisit the stage and market rankings once a year against audience and revenue data.

## How the products fit

- **Daily SITREP (free)**: what moved, the mechanism, where it landed, and who it touches.
- **The Sovereo Brief (paid, weekly)**: one consequential decision, taken through the full chain, with a dated forecast.
- **The Reckoning (forecast ledger)**: every forecast scored in public.
- **The Sovereo Index and country dossiers**: the country layer, scored on the five domains.
- **Free tools**: instruments for specific choices (income rank, cost of living, tax, inflation, currency, debt, passport power, investment climate, country fit). Each is framed as a decision tool, not a moving tool.
- **Advisory and consultation**: applying the chain to one person's situation and countries.

## Evidence standard

- Every consequential claim is sourced. Forecasts are dated and scored.
- Sources are vantage-balanced: primary, regional, and local sources carry the analysis beyond Western wires. State media is marked as framing, never as fact.
- Politically neutral. No country is the hero or the villain; the reader's decision is the subject.

## Global practice

- **Currency**: tools accept and display the reader's own currency. Analysis names which currency a number is in.
- **Units and dates**: write dates as "23 September 2026" or "September 23, 2026," never ambiguous numeric forms. Give metric and imperial where it matters.
- **Language**: plain international English that translates cleanly. Avoid idioms and sports metaphors tied to one country.
- **Not advice**: Sovereo publishes research and education, not personalized financial, tax, legal, immigration, or medical advice in any jurisdiction. Rules differ by country; readers confirm with a licensed professional where they live.
- **Privacy**: design to the strictest regime a reader may fall under (GDPR as the baseline).

## Language rules

- Say "decision," "choice," "tradeoff," "exposure," "what it costs you." Avoid leading with "relocate," "move abroad," "escape," or "exit."
- Name the life stage when it sharpens the point ("If you are within five years of retirement...").
- Name the country exposure when it sharpens the point ("If you earn in pounds and spend in euros...").
- House style holds: no em dashes, no filler, politically neutral, institution tone.
