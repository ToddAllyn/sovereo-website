How many countries can the average Social Security check still get you into?

A fixed retirement income in dollars does not qualify you everywhere. Many countries set a minimum monthly income for a retiree visa. We compare each minimum with the average US Social Security retirement check. A country gets a CLEAR only when the check is at least ten percent above the minimum. The table below lists the countries we have checked against official sources, with the exact coverage ratio (the check divided by the minimum) for each.

We measure against the average retirement check paid by the US Social Security Administration (SSA). It reached $2,085.98 by July 2026. In January 2026, after the yearly 2.8 percent cost-of-living raise, the estimate was $2,071. This is an average, not a median (the middle value), and we say so plainly so anyone can repeat the math. Your own check, and the exchange rate (what a dollar trades for in local money) on the day, change the result. That is why we require a safety margin rather than a simple pass or fail.

Spain at about $2,772 gives 0.75, and Mexico at roughly $4,400 gives about 0.47. Both are clearly Tier 3, nowhere near the line.

How we did it, and what can change

The check we compare against

The check is the average monthly benefit the US Social Security Administration pays retired workers, $2,085.98 as of July 2026. We label it an average because it is one; SSA does not publish a monthly median. Each country's figure is the stated income minimum for its retiree, pensioner, rentista (living off your own income), or independent-means visa. Some minimums are tied to another number, such as a minimum wage. We store those as a formula: Colombia as three times the legal minimum wage, Ecuador as three times the SBU, Portugal as the national minimum wage. When that number changes, ours updates too, so it never goes stale.

The Residency Coverage Ratio

Each checked row shows RCR = 2,085.98 ÷ required monthly income. We mark a country CLEAR only at an RCR of 1.10 or higher. That means a real ten percent cushion, so a small currency swing cannot flip the result. MARGINAL (0.90 to 1.099) marks the rows where exchange rates, the yearly update to the minimum, or a smaller-than-average check could change the answer.

What "verified" means, and what we hold back

Only the ten rows in the first table are tied to a law or an official program, and only they carry a coverage ratio. We cannot yet back the full country list with confirmed official sources, so we publish no total count (for instance "sixteen clear"). A row tied to another number, like a minimum wage, appears only when we have confirmed both the legal formula and that number's current value. Everything else stays in the "still being checked" list, uncounted, until it meets that bar. Currency conversions use the exchange rates on August 12, 2026 (euro at $1.155, Colombian peso at 3,213 per dollar), which is why they are dated.

Qualifying once is not the same as staying qualified

Minimums tied to a local minimum wage (Portugal, Colombia, and Ecuador in the checked list) update every January. A check that qualifies today can fall short next year even if nothing else changes. Minimums set in a currency whose value moves freely, Colombia most of all here, shift with the exchange rate week to week. And even where the check qualifies, local inflation (rising prices) cuts what it buys. The income minimum is the first question, not the whole answer.
