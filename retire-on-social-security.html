How many countries can the average Social Security check still get you into?

A fixed dollar retirement income does not open every door. Each country's retirement-visa income minimum is measured against the average US Social Security retired-worker benefit, and a country earns a CLEAR only when the check carries at least ten percent of headroom over the requirement. The benchmark is the SSA average retired-worker benefit, which reached $2,085.98 by July 2026 (the January 2026 estimate after the 2.8 percent cost-of-living adjustment was $2,071). This is an average, not a median, and the metric is stated as such so the index stays reproducible. A single retiree's own benefit, and the exchange rate on the day, move the result, which is why the classification uses a headroom band rather than a bare pass or fail.

How the classification works

Residency Coverage Ratio
RCR = average retired-worker SS benefit ÷ required monthly income
Tier 1 · CLEARRCR at or above 1.10. At least ten percent of income headroom over the requirement.
Tier 2 · MARGINALRCR 0.90 to 1.099. Exchange-rate moves, annual indexation, or a below-average benefit can change qualification.
Tier 3 · DOES NOT CLEARRCR below 0.90. The requirement sits clearly above the average check.

Colombia at a roughly $1,635 threshold gives an RCR of about 1.28, so the average benefit carries about 28 percent headroom: comfortably Tier 1, not merely qualifying. Spain at about $2,772 gives 0.75, and Mexico at roughly $4,400 gives about 0.47: both clearly Tier 3, nowhere near the line.

Method, sourcing, and the second leg

The benchmark

The check is the US Social Security Administration's average retired-worker benefit, $2,085.98 as of July 2026. This is an average and is labelled as such; SSA does not publish a monthly median. Each country's figure is its retirement, pensioner, rentista, or independent-means route's stated income minimum. Indexed thresholds are stored as a formula and a driver (for example, Colombia as three times the statutory minimum wage, Ecuador as three times the SBU, Portugal as the national minimum wage) so the number recomputes when the driver reprices, rather than sitting as a stale dollar value.

The Residency Coverage Ratio

Each verified row carries RCR = 2,085.98 ÷ required monthly income. A country is published CLEAR only at an RCR of 1.10 or higher, so classification depends on a real ten-percent margin rather than on which side of the benchmark the figure happens to land after a currency wobble. MARGINAL (0.90 to 1.099) flags the rows where exchange rates, annual indexation, or a below-average individual benefit could change the answer.

What "verified" means here, and what is held

Only the ten rows in the first table are anchored to a statute or an official program and carry a coverage ratio. The complete country table is not yet reproducible from confirmed primary sources, so no total count (for instance "sixteen clear") is published. An indexed row is published only when both its statutory formula and its current driver are verified; everything else sits in the in-verification list, uncounted, until it clears that bar. Foreign-exchange conversions use spot rates as of August 12, 2026 (euro at $1.155, Colombian peso at 3,213 per dollar) and are dated for that reason.

Clearing the gate once is not the same as keeping it clear

Thresholds tied to a local wage floor, Portugal, Colombia, and Ecuador among the verified set, reprice every January, so a check that clears today can slip below next year with nothing else changing. Thresholds in a floating currency, Colombia most of all here, move with the exchange rate week to week. And even where the check clears, local inflation erodes what it buys. The income gate is the first question, not the whole one.
