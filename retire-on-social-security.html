Where a Social Security check clears the door

A fixed dollar retirement income does not open every door. For each destination below, the question is simple: does an average US Social Security payment meet the country's retirement-visa income minimum? The answer sorts the world into three groups, and the sourced, dated table shows exactly where your check lands.

Average monthly SS check

The benchmark is the US Social Security Administration's average retired-worker benefit for January 2026, about 2,020 dollars a month. The Administration does not publish a monthly median, which runs lower, near 1,900 dollars, because the benefit distribution is right-skewed. So a threshold under about 1,700 dollars clears on either measure; 1,900 to 2,100 dollars is the knife-edge where average versus median, and the exchange rate, decide it.

Not an income test, or no retirement visa

11 destinations
Gated by a deposit or investment, not monthly income.

Malaysia MM2H (a fixed deposit of $150,000 to $1,000,000 plus a property purchase), Indonesia Second Home (a deposit near $130,000), Seychelles (deposit or asset based), and Malta's Retirement Programme (no income floor; gated by a 7,500 euro minimum tax and a property outlay). A Social Security check is not the relevant test for any of these.

No retirement-income visa exists in 2026.

Georgia (a 365-day visa-free stay for US citizens; residence otherwise needs a $150,000 property purchase), Vietnam, and Australia, where the investor-retirement subclasses 405 and 410 are both repealed to new applicants. A retiree has no income-based route to any of these.

No published income figure.

Morocco, Egypt, Botswana, and Montenegro assess a discretionary sufficient-means test with no gazetted monthly minimum located. Treat any circulating number as unverified until the mission confirms it.

Method, sourcing, and the second leg

The benchmark

The check is the US Social Security Administration's average retired-worker benefit for January 2026, about 2,020 dollars a month, with a median near 1,900 dollars. Figures for each country are the retirement, pensioner, rentista, or independent-means route's stated income minimum, converted to US dollars at spot exchange rates as of August 2026. Where a threshold is a range or is set by consular discretion, that is noted in the row.

How solid each figure is

Rows marked Primary are anchored to a statute or an official program: Portugal's minimum-wage decree, Colombia's minimum-wage decree, and Belize's Qualified Retirement Program, run by the Belize Tourism Board. Rows marked Advisory are compiled from immigration-advisory and consular sources and cross-checked, but are not anchored to a single published statute. Several official government pages could not be verified from source at compile time; those figures rest on corroboration and should be confirmed directly.

Clearing the gate once is not the same as keeping it clear

Thresholds tied to a local wage floor, Portugal, Colombia, Ecuador, and Turkey among them, reprice every January, so a check that clears today can slip below next year with nothing else changing. Thresholds in a floating currency, Colombia, Argentina, South Africa, and Turkey, move with the exchange rate week to week. And even where the check clears, local inflation erodes what it buys. The income gate is the first question, not the whole one.
