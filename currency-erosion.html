How to read this, in plain English

You do not need any finance background. Here is the whole thing, simply.

What does "lost against the dollar" mean?

It means one unit of the currency buys fewer US dollars than it used to. If a currency has lost 50 percent against the dollar, money kept in it now converts to half as many dollars as it did then. For anyone who earns, saves, or spends across borders, that is a real cut in what your money can buy.

Why currencies lose value

Mostly heavy money printing, large government deficits (spending more than it collects in taxes), high inflation (fast-rising prices), or investors and savers losing confidence. Currencies of strong, stable economies tend to hold their value. Those under strain tend to lose value, sometimes slowly, sometimes all at once.

Why it matters for your life

If your income and savings are in a currency that is losing value, you get poorer compared with the rest of the world, even if the number on your paycheck rises. It is one quiet reason people shift income and savings toward steadier currencies.

Where this comes from. Exchange rates are pulled live from the Frankfurter service, which publishes European Central Bank reference rates, with no key required. Change is measured from the reference rate on or just before the same calendar date one, five, and ten years ago to the latest published rate, expressed as the change in the currency's value in US dollars. The service covers around thirty widely traded currencies; some currencies that swing wildly or face government limits are not included. Rates are guide values, not the price at any particular bank or exchange. For general education, not financial advice.
